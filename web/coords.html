geoguessr-ai: coordinates

Upload a street-level photo; the model guesses where on Earth it was taken and shows its five most likely areas on a map. It runs in your browser.

Your photo never leaves your device. The model is downloaded once and the prediction is computed locally. The map tiles come from OpenStreetMap, so OpenStreetMap's servers see which area of the map is shown.

The model download is large (about 350 MB), and one prediction can take tens of seconds. A desktop computer is recommended.

    How to read this. The world is divided into 5000 areas (cells: small where OpenStreetView-5M has many photos, large where it has few). The model scores every cell; each marker is the center of a cell, not an exact spot. The percentages are the model's softmax outputs, not calibrated confidence.

    This is the cell classifier from Phase 3 of the project, not its best predictor (the hybrid, which also searches 500,000 training images, is too large to run in a browser).

    StreetCLIP image encoder (Haas et al. 2023, CC BY-NC 4.0) and a linear head over geographic cells trained on OpenStreetView-5M (CC BY-SA 4.0), run with onnxruntime-web. Non-commercial use only. Files: Daggerabuse/geoguessr-streetclip-geocells (8-bit, use fp32). Preprocessing as for the training embeddings: short side 336 (bicubic), one center crop, no flips.
